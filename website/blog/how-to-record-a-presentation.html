How to Record a Presentation With Your Webcam

Record a presentation with webcam and voice in Chrome: Google Slides presenter view or slideshow, a camera bubble, pauses between sections and an MP4 file.

Shahriar AhmedDeveloper of FrameShotNovember 16, 20268 min read
FrameShot Studio

To record a presentation with your webcam, open your slides in a Chrome tab, start a screen recorder with your microphone and a camera bubble switched on, share the tab with your slideshow, and present as you normally would. You end up with one video that shows your slides, your face in a corner and your voice, ready to send as an MP4.

Below you’ll find how to prepare the slides, whether to record presenter view or the slideshow, the exact steps in Chrome, how to handle pauses and mistakes, and what to do with the file afterwards. The examples use Google Slides, but the same approach works for any presentation that runs in a browser tab.

How to prepare slides for a recorded presentation

A recorded presentation is watched differently from a live one. People watch on laptops and phones, often at a smaller size, and they can pause or skip. A few changes help:

  • Use a widescreen slide size. A 16:9 deck fills a standard video frame with no black bars. In Google Slides, the page size is under File → Page setup.
  • Make text bigger than usual. If a line is hard to read on your laptop at arm’s length, it will be unreadable on a phone. Fewer words per slide helps more than any font.
  • Leave a corner free. Your camera bubble will sit in one corner of the video. Pick a corner, usually bottom right, and keep important content away from it.
  • Write speaker notes. A few bullets per slide keep you on track without reading a script word for word.
  • Check embedded videos and links. If a slide plays a video with sound, you’ll want to record the tab’s sound too.
  • Remove anything private. Comments, draft notes and real customer names have a way of showing up in recordings. Fixing them afterwards means editing the video.

Should you record presenter view or the slideshow?

Google Slides has two ways to present, and the difference matters when you record:

  • Slideshow shows your slides full screen in the tab. Nothing else is visible.
  • Presenter view shows the slideshow in the tab as well, and opens a separate small window with your speaker notes, a timer and the next slide.

Either way, the thing to record is the tab with the slides, never the presenter view window. Viewers should see your slides, not your notes and timer.

What you shareWhat viewers seeWhen to use it
The slideshow tabOnly the slidesAlmost always; it also lets you record sound from embedded videos
The presenter view windowYour notes, timer and next slideNever for the audience
Entire screenSlides plus anything else on that screenWhen you switch between the slides and a live demo in another app

With two monitors, the best setup is the slideshow full screen on one and the presenter view window on the other. With one screen, the notes window can still sit to the side, outside the recorded tab. If you present from a desktop app instead of a browser, share your entire screen, since window recording never includes sound and full-screen apps can behave differently from one system to another.

Set up your webcam, microphone and room

Your voice matters more than your picture. Viewers forgive a slightly soft camera, but not a muffled or echoing voice.

  • Microphone: a headset or a microphone close to your mouth, in a quiet room. Soft furnishings cut down echo.
  • Camera: roughly at eye level, so you aren’t looking down at the lens. A stack of books under a laptop works.
  • Light: face a window or a lamp. Light behind you turns your face into a silhouette.
  • Background: tidy and plain. The bubble is small, but a busy background still pulls attention.
  • Notifications: turn on Do Not Disturb on Windows, or a Focus on a Mac. Recording only the tab keeps pop-ups out of the video, but they still distract you.
  • Headphones: if your slides play sound, wear them so your microphone doesn’t record the same sound a second time.

Look at the camera, not the slides, when you open and close. It feels odd, but it reads as eye contact on the recording.

How to record a presentation with webcam in Chrome

Here is the process with FrameShot’s screen recorder for Chrome:

  1. Open your slides in a Chrome tab and go to the first slide, but don’t start the slideshow yet.
  2. Click the FrameShot icon and choose Record screen. The recorder opens in a FrameShot tab, which must stay open while you record.
  3. Switch on the microphone and check the level meter as you speak. Chrome asks for permission the first time.
  4. Switch on the camera bubble. Choose the corner you left free on your slides and one of the three sizes. Your camera appears in a round bubble that is drawn into the video.
  5. Turn on tab sound if any slide plays a video or audio.
  6. Choose quality and a countdown. 1080p at 30 frames per second suits most decks. A 5 or 10 second countdown gives you time to start the slideshow.
  7. Click Start recording. In Chrome’s dialog, choose the tab with your slides and click Share. Chrome usually switches to that tab.
  8. Start the slideshow during the countdown. In Google Slides, click Slideshow, or open its menu for presenter view.
  9. Present. Talk to the camera, move through your slides, and pause between sections if you need a moment.
  10. Stop when you’re done. End the slideshow, then stop from the FrameShot popup, the recorder tab or Chrome’s Stop sharing bar. The video opens in the player.

You can move or resize the camera bubble while recording, for example if a slide has a chart in that corner. The same setup works for a lesson or a product walkthrough; our tutorial video guide focuses on that.

Pauses, mistakes and keeping it short

Pause between sections. FrameShot’s pause leaves the paused time out of the video, so you can stop to breathe, check your notes or drink some water without a long silence in the result. The pause button is in the FrameShot popup on the toolbar and in the recorder tab.

A full-screen slideshow hides the toolbar. A few ways around that:

  • Use a second monitor with a normal Chrome window on it, so the toolbar is always within reach.
  • Leave the slideshow with Esc at the end of a section, pause, and start the slideshow again from the current slide when you resume. The viewer sees a clean jump to the next section.
  • Record each section separately. Stop at the end of each part and start a new recording for the next. You get several short videos instead of one long one.

Handle mistakes calmly. A small slip is fine: correct yourself and carry on, as you would live. If a take goes badly wrong early on, discard it and start again. Discarding deletes all the recorded data.

Keep it short. Recorded presentations tend to run longer than live ones because there’s nobody to keep you moving. Cut slides rather than speeding up, and keep each recording to one topic. If you have an hour of material, several short parts are easier to watch than one long video.

FrameShot doesn’t edit or trim videos. The only range you can choose is when you export a GIF. To cut out a section, join parts or add titles, download the MP4 and open it in a video editor.

Which quality settings suit a slide presentation?

FrameShot records at 720p, 1080p, 1440p or 4K, at 15, 30 or 60 frames per second. Quality is the largest size recorded: a smaller tab or screen keeps its own size and isn’t scaled up.

PresentationSuggested setting
Standard slides with text and images1080p, 30 fps
Dense charts, spreadsheets or code on a large monitor1440p, 30 fps
Long talks where file size matters1080p or 720p, 15 fps
Slides with smooth animations or embedded video1080p, 30 or 60 fps

Slides change rarely, so a low frame rate looks fine for the slides themselves. Your face in the bubble moves all the time, though, and at 15 fps it can look a little choppy. 30 fps is a safe middle. For more ways to keep the file manageable, see how to reduce screen recording file size.

Export the MP4 and make a GIF of a key slide

When you stop, the recording is saved in FrameShot’s library on your computer and opens in the player. From there:

  • Download the video. It is saved as MP4 when your Chrome can record it, which plays in presentation apps, on phones and on video sites, and as WebM otherwise. Our guide to MP4 vs WebM explains why.
  • Rename it with the talk’s name and date, and mark it as a favourite if you’ll reuse it.
  • Save frame turns the current moment into a screenshot, opened in the editor. It makes a good thumbnail or a still for an email.
  • Export GIF turns a short range, up to 60 seconds, into a looping GIF. Pick a width between 320 and 800 pixels and a frame rate between 10 and 20 fps.

A GIF works well for one key moment: a slide transition that reveals a result, a chart building up, or a before-and-after. It plays by itself in docs, chats and email, without sound. Keep it to a few seconds for a small file; our guide to turning a screen recording into a GIF has the details.

Nothing is uploaded at any point, as our privacy policy explains. You share the video by sending or uploading the downloaded file wherever your audience will watch it.

Common questions

How do I record myself presenting a PowerPoint or Google Slides deck?

Open the deck in a Chrome tab, start a screen recorder with the microphone and camera on, share that tab and start the slideshow. For a desktop presentation app, share your entire screen instead.

Can I see my speaker notes while recording?

Yes. In Google Slides, use presenter view and record only the slideshow tab. The notes window isn’t part of the tab, so viewers don’t see it.

Will the webcam bubble cover my slides?

It sits in one corner. Choose the corner and size before you start, keep that corner of your slides clear, and move or resize the bubble while recording if a slide needs the space.

Can I edit the recording in FrameShot?

No. FrameShot records, plays and downloads videos but doesn’t edit them, apart from choosing a range for a GIF. Pause while recording to leave out gaps, and use a video editor for cuts.

How do I share a recorded presentation?

Download the MP4 and upload it to your video platform, course site or shared drive, or attach it if it is small enough. Recording a live talk in a call instead? See how to record a Google Meet call.
