How to Screenshot Part of a Web Page in Chrome

Screenshot part of a web page or capture a single element in Chrome: system snipping tools, DevTools commands and an extension that scrolls past the screen.

Shahriar AhmedDeveloper of FrameShotNovember 14, 20268 min read
FrameShot Studio

To screenshot part of a web page, drag a box over the area you want with your computer’s snipping shortcut, use the Capture node screenshot command in Chrome DevTools to grab one element, or use an extension that can select an area and keep going past the bottom of the screen. The first is fastest, the second is the most precise, and the third is the only one that handles a part that is taller than your screen.

This guide walks through all of them, then covers the awkward cases: long tables, comment threads, charts with tooltips, and partial screenshots that come out blurry.

How do you screenshot part of the screen on Windows, Mac and Chromebook?

Every desktop system has a built-in way to drag a box around part of the screen. They don’t know anything about web pages, so they capture exactly the pixels you can see, but for a quick grab that is often all you need.

  • Windows: press Win+Shift+S. The screen dims and a small bar at the top lets you choose a rectangle, a freeform shape, a window or the full screen. Drag your box and the image goes to the clipboard. Click the notification that appears to open it in Snipping Tool, where you can mark it up and save it.
  • Mac: press Cmd+Shift+4 and drag. The screenshot is saved to your desktop by default. Hold Control while you drag to copy it to the clipboard instead, or press Cmd+Shift+5 for a toolbar with more options, such as where to save.
  • Chromebook: press Ctrl+Shift and the Show windows key, then drag. Our Chromebook screenshot guide covers the other modes and keyboards without that key.

The limits are the same everywhere. You can only capture what fits on the screen at that moment, your selection is only as accurate as your hand, and the image has the resolution of your display, nothing more. If you need the visible screen as a whole, our guide on how to take a screenshot in Chrome compares the options.

How to capture an element with Chrome DevTools

Chrome’s developer tools can screenshot a single part of a page exactly to its edges, with no extension. It sounds technical, but it takes four steps:

  1. Right-click the thing you want, such as a pricing card or a comment, and choose Inspect. DevTools opens with that part of the page selected in the Elements panel.
  2. Check the selection. As you hover over lines in the Elements panel, Chrome highlights the matching part of the page. If only a word or an icon is highlighted, click the line above it to move up to the box that contains it.
  3. Right-click the selected line and choose Capture node screenshot.
  4. Find the PNG in your Downloads folder.

A node screenshot usually includes the whole element, even the parts scrolled out of view, which makes it handy for a long card or form. For a free-form area instead, open the Command Menu with Ctrl+Shift+P (Cmd+Shift+P on a Mac), type screenshot and choose Capture area screenshot, then drag a box over the page.

The downsides: finding the right line in the Elements panel can take a few tries on complicated pages, there is no preview before the file is saved, and you need another app to crop, annotate or blur the result.

How to screenshot a selected area, even past the screen

FrameShot, a screenshot and screen recording extension for Chrome, has a Selected area mode that works like a snipping tool inside the page, with one big difference: you can scroll while you select, and the capture grows with you.

  1. Click the FrameShot icon and choose Selected area, or press Alt+Shift+A (Option+Shift+A on a Mac).
  2. Drag a box over the part you want.
  3. Adjust it. Drag the handles to resize, drag the middle to move it, or use the arrow keys for small nudges.
  4. Need more? Scroll the page down. The selection extends beyond the screen, and FrameShot captures and stitches the extra part for you.
  5. Press Enter to capture, or Esc to cancel. The image opens in the editor, where you can crop, annotate and blur it, then download it or copy it to the clipboard.

That scroll-to-extend trick is what makes partial captures of long sections practical: a full set of search results, a long settings form or a whole article without the sidebar, all in one clean image. If you want everything from top to bottom instead, a full-page screenshot is quicker, and our guide to scrolling screenshots explains the other ways to capture more than one screen.

How to capture an element exactly to its edges

When the part you want is one block on the page, such as a chart, a product card, a tweet-style post or a single comment, dragging a box by hand always leaves a sliver of background on one side. FrameShot’s Element mode captures it to the exact pixel instead.

  1. Choose Element in the popup, or press Alt+Shift+E (Option+Shift+E on a Mac).
  2. Hover over the page. FrameShot highlights the element under your pointer.
  3. Fine-tune with the arrow keys. Press ↑ to step out to the parent element, for example from one comment to the whole thread, and ↓ to step back in.
  4. Click to capture. The result opens in the editor.

Element mode is the no-DevTools version of a node screenshot. It is the quickest way to get tidy, consistent images of interface parts for documentation or a design review.

If you only ever need a quick grab of what’s on screen, the system shortcut is hard to beat. If you capture parts of web pages often, for tickets, docs or reviews, an in-page tool saves the time you would otherwise spend cropping.

Tips for tables, comments and charts

  • Long tables. If the whole page scrolls, use Selected area and scroll down to the last row. If the table scrolls inside its own box, choose Scrolling panel in the FrameShot popup and click the table: FrameShot captures the panel’s full content, not just the rows you can see.
  • Comment threads. Use Element on one comment, then press ↑ until the highlight covers the replies too. Expand collapsed replies before you start.
  • Charts. Wait for the chart’s entry animation to finish before capturing. For a tooltip that only appears while you hover, use Visible area with a 3, 5 or 10 second delay, move your pointer onto the chart, and crop the result in the editor.
  • Menus and dropdowns. These close as soon as you click anything, including a capture button. The delayed Visible area capture solves this too.
  • Cookie banners and chat bubbles. Close them first, or they may sit right on top of the part you want.
  • Private details. A partial screenshot can still include a name or an email address at its edge. Check before you share, and see our guide to blurring and redacting screenshots.

No extension can capture Chrome’s own pages, such as chrome://settings, the Chrome Web Store or other extensions’ pages. Chrome blocks them for security. A system snipping tool still works there.

How to keep a partial screenshot sharp

Small captures blur easily, because they contain few pixels to begin with. A few habits keep them crisp:

  • Capture at your screen’s full resolution. FrameShot captures at your display’s full sharpness, so on a high-resolution screen with twice the usual pixel density a 300-pixel-wide card becomes a 600-pixel-wide image with crisp text.
  • Zoom the page in before you capture when you need a small element bigger. Use Ctrl++ (Cmd++ on a Mac), then capture. Set the zoom first: changing it during a capture interrupts it, and FrameShot tells you if that happens.
  • Save text as PNG. JPEG blurs the edges of letters and lines. Use PNG for interface screenshots, and JPEG or WebP only when file size matters more. Our guide to saving a web page as a PDF or image compares the formats.
  • Crop, don’t shrink. If the image is too big, crop away what you don’t need instead of scaling the whole thing down.
  • Don’t screenshot a screenshot. Capturing an image of an image loses sharpness each time. Go back to the page and capture it again.

Common questions

How do I screenshot only part of a page in Chrome?

Press Win+Shift+S on Windows or Cmd+Shift+4 on a Mac and drag a box, or use an extension’s area mode. In FrameShot, press Alt+Shift+A, drag a box and press Enter.

Can I screenshot a part of a page that’s bigger than my screen?

Yes, but not with system tools. In FrameShot’s Selected area mode, scroll the page while selecting and the capture extends. DevTools’ Capture node screenshot also usually captures a whole element, even the off-screen part.

How do I screenshot a specific element without an extension?

Right-click it, choose Inspect, then right-click the highlighted line in the Elements panel and choose Capture node screenshot. Chrome saves a PNG to your Downloads folder.

Why does my partial screenshot look blurry?

Usually because it was saved as JPEG, scaled down, or captured from a low-resolution screen. Save as PNG, crop instead of resizing, and zoom the page in before capturing small elements.

How do I copy a partial screenshot straight to the clipboard?

On Windows, Win+Shift+S copies automatically. On a Mac, hold Control while dragging. In FrameShot, use Copy in the editor to put the image on the clipboard as a PNG, ready to paste into a chat or a bug report. Want to point at something first? See how to annotate a screenshot.
