MP4 vs WebM for Screen Recordings: Which to Use

MP4 vs WebM explained for screen recordings: codecs, where each file plays and uploads, quality and size, when Chrome records MP4, and how to convert.

Shahriar AhmedDeveloper of FrameShotNovember 16, 20267 min read
FrameShot Studio

MP4 vs WebM comes down to where the video will be played. MP4 with H.264 video plays almost everywhere: phones, Macs, PCs, presentation apps and video editors. WebM plays in web browsers and is an open, royalty-free format, but some desktop apps and editors don’t open it. For a screen recording you’ll share, MP4 is usually the safer choice; WebM is fine when the video will be watched in a browser.

This guide explains what each format actually is, compares compatibility, quality and file size, explains when Chrome can record MP4, and shows how to convert one into the other when you need to.

Containers and codecs: what MP4 and WebM really are

MP4 and WebM are containers: file formats that hold a video track, a sound track and some information about them, such as the length. The actual picture and sound inside are compressed with codecs. When people say a file “won’t play”, the codec is often the real problem, not the container.

  • MP4 is a widely used container from the MPEG family. It most often holds H.264 video and AAC sound, a combination almost every device can play. It can also hold newer codecs such as H.265 (HEVC) or AV1, and Opus sound.
  • WebM is an open container designed for the web and promoted by Google. It holds VP8, VP9 or AV1 video and Opus or Vorbis sound, all of which are royalty-free.

So “MP4 vs WebM” for a screen recording usually means H.264 with AAC or Opus against VP9 or VP8 with Opus. That is exactly what Chrome records.

Where do MP4 and WebM files play and upload?

MP4 is the universal option. An H.264 MP4 opens by double-clicking on Windows and Mac, plays on iPhones and Android phones, drops into presentation software, imports into practically any video editor and uploads to any video site, learning platform or help centre. If you don’t know who will watch your recording or on what, choose MP4.

WebM is at home in the browser. Chrome, Firefox and Edge play it, and recent versions of Safari play many WebM files too. It works well for videos embedded in web pages or shared through tools that play video in the browser. The trouble starts outside the browser:

  • QuickTime Player on a Mac doesn’t open WebM files.
  • Some editors, slide apps and older media players don’t import it, or import the picture without the sound.
  • Some chat and email apps preview MP4 inline but show WebM as a plain file attachment.
  • Older iPhones and iPads may not play it at all.

Sound is a small extra wrinkle. MP4 files normally carry AAC sound. Some carry Opus instead, and while most modern players handle that, a few older apps and editors play the picture silently. If an MP4 plays with no sound in one app but fine in Chrome, that’s the likely cause.

Is WebM better quality or smaller than MP4?

Not by itself. Size depends mainly on the bitrate, the amount of data used for each second of video, and that is chosen by the recorder or the encoder, not by the container. Two files with the same bitrate are about the same size whether they are MP4 or WebM.

What differs is how much quality each codec gets out of that data:

  • VP9, the usual WebM codec, is more modern than H.264 and can look a little sharper at the same file size, especially in scenes with lots of motion.
  • H.264, the usual MP4 codec, is older but extremely well supported, and computers and phones can often decode it in hardware, which saves battery when playing.
  • For screen recordings, where much of the picture is text and interface that barely changes, the difference is usually small. Resolution and frame rate change the size far more than the format does.

In FrameShot, the bitrate is picked from the resolution and frame rate you choose, so MP4 and WebM recordings at the same settings come out at a broadly similar size. When can Chrome record MP4 instead of WebM?

Browser-based recorders, including extensions, record with Chrome’s built-in media recorder. For a long time it could only write WebM, which is why so many browser recordings ended up as .webm files. Newer versions can write MP4 too:

  • Chrome 126 or later can record MP4, when your system has an H.264 encoder that Chrome can use. Where it doesn’t, recordings fall back to WebM.
  • MP4 sound is AAC where Chrome can encode it, which is the case on Windows and macOS, and Opus elsewhere.
  • WebM uses VP9 or VP8 video with Opus sound.

To check your version, open Chrome’s menu and go to Help → About Google Chrome. Chrome checks for updates on that page, and you can restart to finish updating.

One older annoyance is worth knowing about: WebM files written by browsers have often lacked a proper length in their header, so players couldn’t show the duration or let you jump ahead. FrameShot repairs this before saving, so its WebM files show their length and seek normally in every player.

How to choose MP4 or WebM in FrameShot

FrameShot records MP4 with H.264 by default and switches to WebM only when Chrome can’t write MP4. To choose for yourself:

  1. For every recording: open FrameShot’s Settings, go to Screen recording and set Format to MP4 or WebM.
  2. For one recording: click the FrameShot icon, choose Record screen and change Format on the recorder screen before you start. FrameShot remembers your last choice.
  3. Check the result: if your browser can’t write MP4, the recorder says so before you start, and saves WebM instead.

How to convert WebM to MP4 (and back)

If you already have a WebM and need an MP4, you’ll need a converter. Many video editors, media players and dedicated converter apps can open a WebM and export it as MP4. The general steps are the same in most of them:

  1. Open or import the WebM file.
  2. Choose MP4 as the output, with H.264 video and AAC sound for the widest compatibility.
  3. Keep the resolution and frame rate the same as the original unless you also want to shrink the file.
  4. Pick a quality setting that is high enough. Converting re-compresses the video, so each conversion loses a little detail. Convert once, from the original, not from an earlier copy.
  5. Play the new file all the way through, including the sound, before you delete the original.

Converting MP4 to WebM works the same way, with VP9 and Opus as the output codecs.

Be careful with converter websites. To convert your video they usually upload it to their server, and a screen recording can show emails, customer data and your voice. For anything private, use an app that converts on your own computer. The easiest conversion is the one you never need: if you know the file is going to a slide deck, an editor or an iPhone, record MP4 in the first place.

Common questions

Is MP4 or WebM better for screen recordings?

MP4 for anything you share or edit, because it plays almost everywhere. WebM is fine for videos watched in a browser, and it is open and royalty-free. Quality at the same size is similar for typical screen content.

Why does my screen recording save as WebM?

Your browser probably can’t write MP4. Chrome records MP4 from version 126 when an H.264 encoder is available; otherwise recorders fall back to WebM. Update Chrome and try again.

Can I play a WebM file on a Mac or iPhone?

In Chrome, Firefox and recent Safari, usually yes. QuickTime Player doesn’t open WebM, and support on older iPhones and iPads is patchy. Convert it to MP4 if it needs to play everywhere.

Does converting WebM to MP4 reduce quality?

A little, because the video is compressed again. Choose a high quality setting, keep the same resolution, and convert from the original file only once.

Which format should I use for a GIF?

Neither: a GIF is its own image format, made from part of a recording. It suits short silent loops. See how to turn a screen recording into a GIF, and how to record a presentation for an example of when MP4 matters.
